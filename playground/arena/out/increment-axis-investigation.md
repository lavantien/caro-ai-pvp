## q1, the hard-1 collapse

The collapse decomposes into three facts.

First, the mutuals. hard-1 never loses the hard seat set at any control: 3-1-1 at 2+1, 3-2-1 at 3+2, 4-2-0 at 1+0, series tally never behind (s1-0, s1-1, s2-0). Combined 10-5-2 from hard-1's view, a 30 percent two-sided fair-coin outcome (the combined master mutual, master-2 10-5, is the same number). This excludes a large hard-2 edge but cannot exclude a modest one: 17 games carry almost no power against 55/45.

Second, telemetry parity. Depth, time, node rate, and node means for the two hard seats sit within measurement noise of each other at all three controls (2+1: 9.2 vs 8.9 mean depth, 5.79m vs 5.76m nps; 3+2: 9.2 vs 8.9, 4.96m vs 4.51m; 1+0: 7.4 vs 7.5, 4.49m vs 4.62m). At 3+2 hard-2 posted the lower nps yet the better record. There is no resource, contention, or clock asymmetry between the seats.

Third, the gap's origin and its drift. The rating gap is built entirely from cross-tier series, and the tier that beats hard-1 changes with the control: at 2+1 hard-1 loses the easy aggregate 3-7-1 while hard-2 holds 6-3-2; at 3+2 hard-1 loses the medium aggregate 2-7-1 (hard-2: 5-5-1) while winning the master aggregate 6-4-1, including 4-1 over master-1; at 1+0 hard-1 is positive against everything below master (8-3 medium, 6-5 easy). A large stable deficit produces a stable victim ordering at these sample sizes; a modest one does not, because each seat-versus-tier aggregate holds roughly 10 decisive games. The observed drift rules out the first class and is silent on the second.

The direct test on the record where a hard-1 deficit would have to appear: pooled over the two increment arms, hard-1 went 13-21 in decisive games against easy and medium (6-12 at 2+1, 7-9 at 3+2), a 23 percent two-sided fair-coin outcome. The weaker-seat reading fails to separate from noise, and so would its opposite.

Loss shape is descriptive only and carries no differential weight: the -M2-own versus M1-winner ending is common to essentially every decisive game for every seat at every control (20 of hard-1's 21 losses at 2+1 and all 15 at 3+2, with the winner at M1 in every decisive game in every arm), so it discriminates nothing between seats. What it establishes is the absence of the failure modes that would implicate time handling: no game in any arm ended outside the three win classifications (4 with 361, open 4 with 34, double 4 with 6 over the 401 decisive games), and no loss carries a clock-exhaustion shape; the exceptional endings (one -52900 leaf at 2+1, nine 1+0 losses at -7200 to -24200) sit in the ordinary lost-position range.

Verdict: no large stable hard-tier defect is demonstrable, the direct tests fail to separate from noise in both directions, and hard-1 won every mutual set. The 204-point and 166-point gaps are schedule-slot variance under a bo3 double round robin where each pairing contributes roughly 5 games, amplified by the first-player effect below. A modest or control-local deficit is not excluded at these sample sizes; that is a power statement, not a suspicion.

## q2, master-1's losses to hard at 3+2

master-1 went 1-8 against the hard seats at 3+2 (and 5-7 in series overall, 13-17-4 in games, with 4 draws against easies alone) while master-2 went 7-3-1 and 10-2. The same binary at 2+1 was the top seat of the whole field (12-2, 8-0 against hard) and at 1+0 stands 5-4 against hard with the second-best rating. The master mutual flips control by control: master-1 3-2 at 2+1, master-2 4-1 at both 3+2 and 1+0.

One contamination must be disclosed before reading this: the 1-8 includes two games from s31, the series the external kill removed mid-run and the resume replayed whole. The clean sub-record is 1-6, and the worst cell of the anomaly overlaps the one documented machine incident at this control.

The mechanism question has no persistent-instance answer available: searchers are rebuilt with fresh clocks every game, so the seat label is a schedule slot plus a hosting pattern, not an engine carrying form between games or controls. What the flip across controls measures is game-level noise plus any control-dependent effect on one slot, and with one arm per control the two cannot be told apart. The 2 percent one-sided tail on 1-8 (over 9 games, the raw record; the clean 1-6 is a 6 percent tail) bounds only the noise component. Game-level detail adds nothing either way: all eight losses end -M2 versus M1 like every other seat's losses, over game lengths from 21 to 142 moves.

Verdict: not established as a tier anomaly and not attributable to any identified mechanism; a single-arm anomaly whose clean sub-record still leans wrong but cannot separate, sitting at n=1 controls. v0.28 is unaffected by construction, its gate specified over pooled W+D records across seats and both colors. The preregistered discriminator is a second 3+2 arm, which follow-up 2 now carries instead of deferring it.

## q3, mediums below easies at 1+0

The standings inversion is real (easy-1 939 over medium-1 846 and medium-2 795) but the direct matchup is not inverted: mediums beat easies 10-8-1 at 1+0. The direct edge is inside noise at every control (11-5-5 at 2+1, 11-7-4 at 3+2, 10-8-1 at 1+0, none separating), so an edge that shrinks with speed is not measurable either; what the control actually changes is game character, chiefly the draw rate (10.3 and 11.6 percent with increment, 0.7 at 1+0) and game length.

The inversion is built from three components, none a medium capability loss. First, seat noise inside both tiers: easy-1 went 6-3 against mediums while easy-2 went 2-7, and medium-2 collapsed to 13-22 overall. Second, a small easy lean against common upper-tier opposition: against hard, easies 9-14 to mediums 7-14; against master, easies 7-13 to mediums 6-14; 3 net games over 48, itself far inside noise but directionally what the standings amplified. Third, the mean-zero color lottery, which widens standings spreads without favoring either tier, so it cannot by itself put both easies above both mediums.

Depth telemetry cannot carry any of this. Medium-easy depth parity (1+0 means 6.4 and 6.9 versus 6.7 and 6.1) and the roughly 2x medium nps ratio are constant across all three controls, so no search-shape quantity varies with the control in a way that explains the control-dependent standings. The depth relation below master was never a ladder at any control; hard out-searches master in depth at both increment controls (9.2 and 8.9 versus 8.4 and 8.5) and the order flips at 1+0 (7.4 and 7.5 versus 8.3 and 7.8), the same control-linked unpredictability q2 shows, predicted by nothing in tier strength.

Verdict: no capability inversion is licensed; the 1+0 standings are noise around a direct edge too thin to measure at any control. The recorded-not-gated status stands.

## the first-player effect

Red is the largest single effect in the dataset, stable across controls: master red records 31-4-2, 25-9-3, 28-5-0 at 2+1, 3+2, 1+0, and every seat's red record dominates its blue record at every arm (1+0 extremes: master-2 red 16-2 against blue 9-9). The double round robin gives each seat both colors against each opponent, so the effect cancels in expectation but roughly halves the effective sample on every pairing. This is the amplifier behind q1 and q2, and it mandates color-paired analysis in every follow-up measurement.

## power accounting for the re-gate

The design conclusion follows from the strongest pooled edge in the whole evidence set. Master over easy at 2+1, the top tier against the bottom, pooled 14-6-2: a 14-6 decisive record is an 11.5 percent two-sided binomial outcome. Every other tier edge measured here is weaker than that. So a gate built from roughly 20 pooled games per tier edge, which is what one 8-seat bo3 double round robin delivers, cannot certify even a 70 percent edge at conventional confidence, and its standings inversions fire on noise by construction. The v0.19 zero-inversion pass at the old sizes was a draw from the same lottery, not evidence the gate had teeth.

Required samples at two-sided 95 percent confidence and 80 percent power: about 194 games per pairing-family to separate 0.60 from 0.50, about 47 to separate 0.70 from 0.50 (one-sided 95 percent gives about 153 and 38). One 3-repetition gate reaches roughly 60 pooled games per adjacent-tier family, which at a true 0.60 edge carries about 45 percent one-sided power: it certifies the 0.70 regime only. The adjacent edges at the re-scaled sizes sit near the 0.60 regime, so full certification needs about 150 games per family, roughly 7 repetitions or 1000-plus games per arm, a multi-night budget that belongs at the closing ladder round, not at every re-gate.

## limitations

The 2+1 arm mixes memory regimes at the schedule seam: series 1 through 28 ran before the guard kill, 29 through 56 under the GOMEMLIMIT bound, and the seam coincides with the hosting-direction flip, so per-seat magnitudes at that control carry a pooled-over regime shading; hard-1 lost cross-tier games in both halves, so the collapse itself does not ride the seam. The binomial readings assume game independence within pairings; fresh engines and clocks per game make it plausible, deterministic opening play could in principle cluster results, and the color-paired block design below partially controls it. The 3+2 master-1 versus hard cell overlaps the externally killed and replayed s31 as disclosed in q2. Ponder-defect reach, if the stop-join suspect is real, could shade any pondering pairing without emitting an inflated t line whenever core headroom exists, so the t-line confinement bounds only the observable.
